Two geometry-based functions: f₁(x, y, D), f₂(x, y, D)

Physical seg2 always bends away from ZED (negative in this model). Each function measures friction margin at its own roll orientation. This is a numerical algebraic function, not an elementary closed-form fit.

Definition

fₖ(x,y,D) = μ_available − μ_required,k(x,y,D)

μ_required,k is the lowest critical friction among the catalog's exact-contact roots in band k that pass joint-limit, object-clearance and self-collision tests.

f > 0: a candidate passes the friction cutoff.
f = 0: friction boundary, excluded by the strict cutoff.
f < 0: more friction is needed.
No catalog candidate: mathematical value −∞; not proof of global impossibility.

The black curve is different

For node3 side–gripper side contact:
δ = −2 atan(1 / 0.5)
q₂ = δ − q₁ / 5.
Intersect the two lines offset by D/2 to get C(q₁,D). This reference curve uses only trigonometry and line intersection, with no polynomial roots or learned coefficients. Only finite-feature, collision-free subarcs are drawn. It describes one contact branch, not the whole band.

Scope and comparison
The Python functions accept continuous x, y and D. This offline preview uses the XY spacing and diameter subset stated above. The legacy JSON fixed roll at +90° and allowed both seg2 signs, so its policy differs from these functions. It also records only one selected solution per location; it is not a complete label of every possible band. Function-only points are additional validated catalog solutions, not necessarily false positives. JSON-only points may reflect omitted branches, numerical root conditioning or the original contact tolerance. The contact-feature catalog includes the observed pairs and their opposite-side counterparts. It remains empirical. No whole-grasp completeness or dynamic stability proof is claimed. X on the plot is −x in the function. The black curve always refers to μ=0.5, even when the slider changes.
